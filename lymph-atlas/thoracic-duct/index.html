Where does the thoracic duct drain?

The thoracic duct returns lymph from both lower limbs, the abdomen, and the left upper body to the venous circulation near the left internal jugular–subclavian junction. The usual exception is the right upper quadrant: the right side of the head and neck, right arm and right thorax. [1]

Trace the route in three steps

01

Collect

Intestinal and lumbar trunks bring lymph toward the abdominal origin of the thoracic duct, often depicted as the cisterna chyli.

02

Ascend

The duct passes from the abdomen into the thorax and continues toward the left base of the neck.

03

Return

Lymph enters the venous circulation. Left upper-body trunks join the terminal drainage pathway. [1]

Why the route matters

Fluid balance. Lymphatic vessels return interstitial fluid and proteins to blood. If drainage is impaired, fluid can accumulate in tissues. The drainage territory helps connect swelling to the affected pathway. [2]

Fat transport. Intestinal lacteals carry absorbed dietary fats into lymph. This lipid-rich lymph is called chyle and travels through the central lymphatic pathway toward venous blood. [2]

Keep the sides straight: the right leg does not drain into the right lymphatic duct. The right-sided exception concerns the upper quadrant.

Practise with two routes

In LymphAtlas, trace a route from the lower limb and then from the right arm. Identify the terminal duct or trunks and the side of venous return. The goal is to explain the territory before naming the endpoint.
