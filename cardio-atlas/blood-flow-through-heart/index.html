One circuit, two pumps

The right ventricle sends blood to the lungs for gas exchange. The left ventricle sends blood into the systemic circulation. Follow the arrows through both sides before returning to the starting point. [1]

The four valves in order

ValveForward flow
TricuspidRight atrium → right ventricle
PulmonaryRight ventricle → pulmonary trunk
MitralLeft atrium → left ventricle
AorticLeft ventricle → aorta

Valves help prevent backward flow. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood toward it. Pulmonary arteries therefore carry oxygen-poor blood, while pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood. [1]

Clinical connections

01

Valve narrowing

Stenosis restricts forward flow through a valve. Identify the chamber just upstream of the narrowed opening. [2]

02

Valve leakage

Regurgitation allows reverse flow through a valve that does not close effectively. Add a backward arrow to the affected connection. [2]

03

Coronary circulation

The heart muscle needs its own blood supply. Coronary arteries branch from the aorta; blood within the chambers is not its arterial supply. [1]

Practise the route

Start with a red blood cell returning from a leg. Trace every chamber and valve until it reaches the aorta. Then repeat from a pulmonary vein. Use the diagram to check the sequence before exploring the animated flow routes.
